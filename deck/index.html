SLIDE 02 Clinical Friction

Problem Statement

Patients requiring continuous or assisted oxygen support depend on cylinders and concentrators with critical monitoring gaps.
FRICTION 01

Limited Real-Time Visibility

Oxygen supply conditions lack automated tracking, relying on periodic visual inspections by busy hospital or home caregivers.

FRICTION 02

Unknown Remaining Runtime

Difficulty estimating remaining oxygen quantity and exact runtime at fluctuating patient flow rates.

FRICTION 03

Unexpected Depletion Risks

High risk of sudden, undetected oxygen interruption during night shifts, transport, or peak ward consumption.

FRICTION 04

Caregiver Workload Burden

Continuous manual monitoring and manual flow adjustments cause severe physical and mental fatigue for family and staff.

Core Need: An integrated system that improves oxygen supply visibility, patient monitoring, and caregiver awareness simultaneously.
SLIDE 03 State of the Art

Existing Systems & Limitations

Why current standalone equipment fails to prevent avoidable clinical emergencies.
STANDALONE CYLINDERS

Mechanical Flow Regulators

Purely analog gauges. No electronic data, no audible low-supply alarms, and zero warning before total pressure collapse.

CONVENTIONAL CONCENTRATORS

Fixed-Rate Output

Deliver fixed flow independent of patient SpO₂ condition. No integration with patient vitals; basic audio alarms trigger only on complete failure.

DISCONNECTED PULSE OXIMETERS

Isolated Monitoring

Pulse oximeters sound localized beeps but have no communication with the oxygen delivery device to adjust flow or alert remote staff.

The Systemic Gap: No current low-cost device integrates oxygen supply telemetry, patient vitals monitoring, and automatic flow adaptation into one unit.
SLIDE 04 Core Value Proposition

Proposed Solution & Value Proposition

"One intelligent system for oxygen supply management, patient monitoring and caregiver alerts, compatible with existing oxygen sources."
PILLAR 01

Supply Telemetry

Real-time oxygen flow, pressure, remaining volume, and approximate runtime prediction.

PILLAR 02

Patient Monitoring

Continuous SpO₂ and pulse-rate monitoring with instant abnormal parameter threshold alerts.

PILLAR 03

Doctor-Prescribed Auto Flow

Automatic flow regulation within validated, clinician-authorized limits with full manual override.

PILLAR 04

Smart Connectivity

Live mobile telemetry, low-supply notifications, and equipment fault detection.

💡 Compatibility First: Integrates with existing oxygen concentrators and cylinders rather than requiring expensive new oxygen generation systems.
SLIDE 05 System Engineering

Working Principle & System Architecture

A multi-layered mechatronics pipeline connecting sensors, controller decision logic, and safety actuators.
1. SENSING SUBSYSTEM

Continuous Inputs

• Medical Oxygen Flow Sensor
• Pressure Transducer
• Continuous SpO₂ & Pulse Rate Probe

2. INTELLIGENT CONTROLLER

ESP32 Logic Engine

• Real-time parameter evaluation against doctor limits
• Runtime prediction mathematical model
• Fault detection & safe failover routines

3. OUTPUT & ACTUATION

Safe Clinical Delivery

• Electrically controlled oxygen-compatible valve
• High-contrast bedside OLED display
• Audible buzzer + WiFi/BLE Mobile App

Safety Principle Enforced: Automatic adjustment operates strictly within clinician limits. Any sensor failure or equipment fault triggers immediate manual failover.
SLIDE 06 Execution Roadmap

Prototype Design & Development Direction

Staged development prioritizing affordability, practical engineering, and oxygen compatibility.
INITIAL SCOPE DECISION

Oxygen Concentrator First

• Initial prototype will focus on oxygen concentrator compatibility for controlled, safe development.
• High-pressure cylinder compatibility follows in subsequent development phases.
• Strictly using rated oxygen-compatible valves and certified flow sensors.

6-PHASE DEVELOPMENT STAGES

Phase 1: Oxygen flow and pressure monitoring
Phase 2: SpO₂ and pulse-rate monitoring
Phase 3: Remaining supply and runtime estimation
Phase 4: Automatic flow adjustment within prescribed limits
Phase 5: Mobile app and notification integration
Phase 6: Safety testing and complete system integration
